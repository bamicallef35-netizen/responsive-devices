# Lineage — the verified facts, with confidence

Everything the device pages lean on, so a claim can be checked before it is repeated.

**Archigram.** Six members: Peter Cook, Warren Chalk, Ron Herron, Dennis Crompton, Michael Webb, David Greene. The magazine began in 1961 and ran to about nine issues into the early 1970s; the practice Archigram Architects worked c. 1970–74, with the end year given variously as 1974 or 1975 (medium confidence on the exact year). Walking City (Herron, 1964), Plug-In City (Cook, 1964) and Computer City (Crompton, 1964) are secure; Instant City is dated 1968, 1969 or 1968–70 depending on the source (medium). RIBA Royal Gold Medal, 2002 (high).

**Gordon Pask.** Musicolour, from 1953 with Robin McKinnon-Wood, running to about 1957–58: a performer's sound converted into light, with thresholds that rose on repetition so the machine stopped responding and pushed the performer toward novelty (high). The Colloquy of Mobiles was shown at *Cybernetic Serendipity*, ICA London, 2 August – 20 October 1968, curated by Jasia Reichardt; sources vary on the build year, 1967–69 (medium). "The Architectural Relevance of Cybernetics", *Architectural Design*, September 1969, pp. 494–496 (the page numbers are medium confidence; re-verify against the issue). Pask worked with Cedric Price and Joan Littlewood on the unbuilt Fun Palace from about 1963; the membership of its Cybernetics Committee is not stated here because the sources are informal (low). A full-scale replica of the Colloquy was built by Paul Pangaro and TJ McLeish in 2018 and is in the ZKM collection (high).

**W. Grey Walter.** Elmer and Elsie, 1948–49, Burden Neurological Institute, Bristol; the species name *Machina speculatrix*; lifelike behaviour — phototaxis, obstacle negotiation, a return to the hutch on a low battery — from a two-valve reflex loop; shown at the Festival of Britain, 1951 (high). See [grey-walter-tortoise](https://github.com/benmicallef/grey-walter-tortoise).

**W. Ross Ashby.** The Homeostat, completed 1948; *Design for a Brain* (article 1948, book 1952); *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956), with variety defined at p. 126 and the Law of Requisite Variety — "only variety can destroy variety" — at p. 207 (high).

**Jakob von Uexküll.** The Umwelt, from *Streifzüge durch die Umwelten von Tieren und Menschen* (1934): an organism's self-world as cut out by its senses and effectors, as distinct from the surroundings an observer sees; the tick is his classic example; English edition 2010 (high). An honest caveat belongs beside the name: his politics in the 1930s are documented and not admirable.

**François Roche, R&Sie(n), New-Territories.** R&Sie was founded in Paris in 1993 by François Roche with Stéphanie Lavaux (partner to 2011) and Gilles Desevedavy (to 1998); Wikipedia gives 1994 (practice CV; CCA fonds AP193 — high). Jean Navarro was a partner from 2000 to 2007 and is credited on the projects of that period; the name R&Sie(n) dates from 2004; [eIf/bʌt/c] was founded with Camille Lacadée in 2011 (high). Built works verified: Hybrid Muscle (The Land, Chiang Mai, February 2002, with Philippe Parreno) and Spidernethewood (Nîmes, 2007). Speculative, exhibited, unbuilt: *I've heard about…*, MAM/ARC at the Couvent des Cordeliers, 7 July – 9 October 2005 (Roche, Lavaux, Navarro, with Benoît Durandin); *Une architecture des humeurs*, Le Laboratoire, Paris, from 22 January 2010 to late April or mid-May (sources differ). Olzweg (FRAC Centre competition, Orléans, 2006) was a joint laureate; the commission went to Jakob+MacFarlane. The Viab machines were prototypes and exhibition apparatus, with robotic design by Stephan Henrich; nothing of Roche's is a deployed construction-robotics system.
