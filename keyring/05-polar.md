# polar — the sky's compass

> **Status: proposal, buildable today, no electronics.** Prices rough, November 2026.

## Proposal

Two discs of polarising film on a keyring, one fixed, one that turns. Look at a patch of blue sky through them and rotate: the sky darkens and brightens with the angle, and the pattern across the sky points at the sun even when the sun is hidden.

## The sense it adds

Polarisation vision. Sunlight scattered by the atmosphere is polarised in a pattern centred on the sun; bees, ants and some birds read it to steer, and Viking navigators are thought to have used a calcite "sunstone" for the same purpose. The human eye is nearly blind to it (Haidinger's brush is faint); a rotating filter makes the pattern plain.

## What is genuinely achievable today

All of it, with no electronics. Linear polarising film (~£2 for a sheet that cuts into many discs) in a small 3D-printed or laser-cut frame (~£1) with a rotating bezel. A single disc is enough; the second disc, fixed, gives a reference so the darkening is easier to see.

## What remains speculative

Navigating by it with any accuracy. Finding the sun's direction to within ten or twenty degrees from the polarisation band is realistic; doing it well is a skill.
