# responsive-devices

A dossier of speculative cybernetic devices — small machines that sense, respond and adapt — in the register of Archigram, Gordon Pask and François Roche. Each one is a proposal grounded in real, cheap, existing parts, with its price list attached. They are proposals, not products.

## Why this register

Archigram was a London group of six architects — Peter Cook, Warren Chalk, Ron Herron, Dennis Crompton, Michael Webb and David Greene — whose magazine ran from 1961 into the early 1970s. Walking City (Herron, 1964), Plug-In City (Cook, 1964), Computer City (Crompton, 1964) and Instant City (c. 1968–70) were mostly never built. Their value was as precise, provocative proposals, drawn well enough to be argued with; the group received the RIBA Royal Gold Medal in 2002 for them. Gordon Pask built machines that hold a two-way exchange: Musicolour (from 1953) and the Colloquy of Mobiles (Cybernetic Serendipity, ICA, 1968). François Roche, with R&Sie(n) (founded in Paris in 1993 with Stéphanie Lavaux and Gilles Desevedavy) and later New-Territories and [eIf/bʌt/c], proposes architecture as a protocol run under uncertainty — *I've heard about…* (2005), the Olzweg robot (2006), *Une architecture des humeurs* (2010) — and publishes every project as a scenario: programme, area, cost, team, and a one-paragraph premise. Mosquito Bottleneck (Trinidad, 2003) is the model: West Nile fever detected on the island, a house as a Klein-bottle mosquito trap, about 130 m², 200,000 USD stated. This dossier borrows that scenario format and the Archigram register, which are distinct lineages — 1960s London and 1990s–2010s Paris and Bangkok — and holds both to one discipline: every proposal is tied to a parts list. Two R&Sie(n) works stand as precedents for entries not yet written: Dustyrelief/B_mu (Bangkok, 2002), an electrostatic envelope that accretes the city's airborne particles into a relief, and Hybrid Muscle (Chiang Mai, 2002), electricity stored as counterweights raised by an elephant.

## The rule

Every device states, separately and plainly: the cybernetic idea it foregrounds; what is achievable today with named cheap parts; what remains speculative; a rough bill of materials with a cost; and an honest note on ethics and legality. A claim that could not survive a technical interview does not go in. [`METHOD-AND-ETHICS.md`](METHOD-AND-ETHICS.md) sets out the method, a literal glossary, and the rubric each page has to pass.

Where a working repository exists, the dossier page is the hardware half of a thing whose software half already runs and is tested; the page says exactly which half is which.

## Honesty first

None of these diagnoses, treats or improves any medical or psychological condition. None reads emotions. None sends data anywhere by default. Prices are rough, dated, and move. Where a device touches the body or senses a person, its ethics note says exactly what is sensed, where it goes and how long it lives.
